Origins and early work

Health Records for Everyone (HR4E) was founded in 2010 as a 501(c)(3) charitable organization, inspired by clinicians working in overseas mobile clinics and relief settings, including Banda Aceh after the tsunami and Haiti after the earthquake. As some of these groups transitioned into school health assessments in resource-poor environments, it became clear that portable, structured documentation was essential for understanding what had been accomplished and for planning follow-up care.

During this period, clinicians sometimes encountered paper-based health passports carried by patients, especially in rural and low-resource settings such as mobile clinics in Malawi. These small booklets served as the only longitudinal record patients possessed, allowing them to bring their medical history from one encounter to the next. The idea was simple and powerful: the patient should be able to carry their own health information. HR4E adopted this principle early, recognizing that an electronic version could one day replace paper passports once mobile devices became widespread and secure enough to support the concept.

Working with a small group of university students, HR4E built a prototype framework for an electronic health passport (eHP) based on HL7 CCDs. This early system was adapted for school-based health assessments, where it generated more than 1,700 structured documents during a 2011 deployment. The prototype demonstrated that portable, patient-centered records could be created, returned, and processed effectively, giving sponsoring organizations a clear view of the work accomplished and laying the groundwork for the modern vault and eHP architecture used in hr4e-revisited.

Dormancy and continued technical work

Despite the successful pilot, the founding team was not able to sustain an open-source development community around the prototype, and the organization gradually went dormant. The founder retired from clinical practice but remained deeply involved in reporting and software development, focusing on SQL-based analytics and data transformation.

Beginning in 2024, his work was transformed by the practical use of AI, which he began using daily to craft and fine-tune SQL code and VBA routines to transform spreadsheet data alongside EHR-derived extracts. This period laid much of the technical groundwork for a future revival of HR4E.

AI-driven revival and hr4e-revisited

In June 2026, the founder was inspired by a colleague who was using AI to help write code against Epic via a live FHIR API, using a web-style stack with TypeScript, Vue, and Vuetify. Seeing how well this approach worked, he adopted a similar pattern and, over the course of a few weeks, developed prototypes of two components of the system he had long envisioned for HR4E.

Building on these prototypes, the founder is now working to create a suite of apps and tools that can serve as an open-source foundation for HR4E: vaults, editors/viewers, extractors/loaders, and related components that can be deployed in clinics, schools, and relief settings. In parallel, he is exploring how to formally revive the charitable organization to support this renewed technical and mission-driven work under the banner of hr4e-revisited.
