Foundation Overview

The HR4E Foundation Layer defines the core artifacts, conventions, and architectural principles that make HR4E deployments portable, secure, and interoperable across clinics, schools, and relief settings. It provides a stable base upon which tools such as Studio, Extractor/Loader, Analyzer/Reporter, and mobile apps can be built and extended.

The “hr4e-revisited” relaunch (2026) modernizes the original 2010 HR4E mission using contemporary web technologies, AI-assisted development, and portable vault formats that support both personal and clinic-level workflows.

The Foundation Layer exists to give HR4E a stable, portable architecture that works in clinics, schools, and resource-limited environments. It defines the minimal set of tools and conventions needed to create, carry, synchronize, and analyze structured health records, whether used by clinicians or patients themselves.

Core Components

Core Data Artifact

HR4E centers around a single portable data unit:

  • Vaults — portable, USB-friendly stores of HL7 CCD-format clinical documents. Vaults are the foundation for both clinic workflows and personal electronic health passports (eHPs).

Tools That Operate on Vaults

Surrounding the vault are tools that create, edit, extract, analyze, and support operational workflows:

  • Editor/Viewer — platform-specific apps for editing or viewing clinical documents from vaults.
  • Extractor/Loader — tools to populate vaults from existing EHRs (Epic ± others).
  • Analyzer/Reporter — low-overhead reporting tools for small clinics and franchisees using data recorded in vaults.
  • Biller/Bookkeeper — simple tools to submit/record and track billing transactions for services documented in vaults; the foundation for franchise billing operations.
  • Studio — a configurable web-based environment to access these tools with or without Internet access.

Design Principles

The Foundation Layer is guided by several core principles, each motivated by real-world constraints encountered in rural clinics, school-based assessments, and cross-border care environments:

  • Portability — vaults can be carried by patients or moved between clinics. This supports continuity of care when electronic systems are fragmented or unavailable.
  • Offline-first — tools must work in low-connectivity or no-connectivity environments. Many rural clinics and schools lack reliable Internet access.
  • PHI safety — filenames are PHI-free; encryption protects vault contents between uses. Vaults may be physically carried by patients, so they must remain safe even if lost.
  • Interoperability — CCD-subset documents map cleanly to FHIR-like structures. This supports cross-border care and non-Epic/Epic interoperability (e.g., Ireland/Northern Ireland).
  • Low-overhead deployment — tools must be usable by small clinics and relief teams. HR4E avoids enterprise infrastructure and focuses on lightweight, practical workflows.

What the Foundation Layer Does Not Include

The Foundation Layer intentionally excludes several elements to keep HR4E portable, safe, and usable in resource-limited environments:

  • No centralized EHR — HR4E does not require or provide a cloud-based EHR.
  • No cloud dependency — all tools operate offline-first; cloud sync is optional.
  • No proprietary formats — vaults use open, inspectable CCD-subset documents.
  • No PHI in filenames — vault structure is safe even if physically lost.
  • No enterprise infrastructure — HR4E is designed for small clinics and mobile teams.
  • No requirement for Epic or any specific EHR — Extractor/Loader profiles are configurable.

Electronic Health Passport (eHP)

The HR4E electronic health passport (eHP) is the modern evolution of the paper health passports long used in overseas clinics and mobile relief settings. Paper passports allowed patients to personally carry their medical information between encounters, especially in environments where electronic systems were unavailable or fragmented. The eHP preserves this idea while taking advantage of today’s widespread mobile devices and secure biometric authentication.

Technically, an eHP is a personal HR4E vault carried by the patient. It contains HR4E CCD‑subset documents and optional metadata in vault-index.sqlite, following the same vault specification described in the Vault Spec. What distinguishes an eHP from a clinic vault is how it is secured, synchronized, and used across encounters.

  • Offline-first — eHPs work even when connectivity is unavailable, making them suitable for rural clinics, schools, and relief settings.
  • Not offline-only — whenever connectivity is available, patients and health organizations can synchronize eHP contents to ensure a current backup exists.
  • Biometric security — modern mobile devices provide strong authentication mechanisms (FaceID, TouchID, secure enclaves), enabling eHPs to remain encrypted whenever they are stored between uses.
  • Portable and patient-centered — patients carry their own structured health information, allowing continuity of care across clinics, schools, and relief teams.

As HR4E tools mature, eHPs will support additional workflows such as encounter summaries, immunization tracking, school health assessments, and cross-border care coordination. The sequencing of encryption, decryption, and synchronization is under active development as part of the HR4E Foundation Layer roadmap.

Architecture Diagram

The HR4E architecture centers around vaults as the portable data unit, with tools layered around them to support creation, editing, extraction, aggregation, and reporting. A simplified conceptual diagram is shown below: 

[ HR4E Vault ]
     |
     +-- Editor/Viewer (eHP/Vault)
     |
     +-- Extractor/Loader (EHR → Vault)
     |
     +-- Analyzer/Reporter (Vault → Reports)
     |
     +-- Biller/Bookkeeper (Vault → Ledger)

Each component is designed to operate independently or as part of an integrated workflow.

Relationship to the 2010 HR4E Mission

HR4E was founded in 2010 to support clinicians working in overseas mobile clinics and relief settings. The original prototype generated HL7 CCDs for documenting school health assessments and produced more than 1,700 structured documents during a 2011 deployment.

The Foundation Layer preserves the spirit of that mission — portable, structured documentation for resource-poor settings — while modernizing the technical approach using AI-assisted development, web technologies, and secure vault formats.

For a full historical narrative, see the HR4E History page.

Roadmap

The HR4E Foundation Layer will evolve over time as tools mature and pilot deployments expand. Current roadmap items include:

  • Mobile Editor/Viewer apps (iOS first, then Android) supporting offline vault creation and USB-based eHP workflows.
  • HIPAA-safe Extractor/Loader using Azure OpenAI and configurable EHR profiles.
  • Franchise-ready billing tools for small +/- mobile clinics and microbusinesses in resource-poor settings.
  • Pilot deployments in efforts for supporting care between non-Epic and Epic-based sites.
  • Expanded documentation including developer guides, API references, and internationalization support.
